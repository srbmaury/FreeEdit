# The Tiny Mirror That Measures the Moon

## Sources

- Apollo 11 lunar surface photograph (AS11-40-5942), showing astronaut Buzz Aldrin with the lunar experiment package; photographed by Neil Armstrong. [NASA image record](https://images.nasa.gov/details-as11-40-5942)
- Earth–Moon orbital visualization: NASA Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio. [NASA media page](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/earth-and-moon/)
- Lunar laser ranging background, reflectors left during Apollo, pulse-return timing, ~2.5-second round trip, and the Moon's recession rate: [NASA/JPL](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/the-apollo-experiment-that-keeps-on-giving/)
- Photon return example: NASA reports about 200 photons returned from tens of thousands of pulses in an LRO reflector experiment. [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/laser-beams-reflected-between-earth-and-moon-boost-science/)

## YouTube metadata draft

**Title:** Apollo Left a Mirror on the Moon. It Still Works.

**Description:** Apollo-era reflectors still help scientists measure the Earth–Moon distance. A laser round trip takes about 2.5 seconds, and the timing can measure distance to a few millimeters. One NASA orbiter test received only about 200 returning photons from tens of thousands of pulses. Visuals: NASA / Neil Armstrong; NASA Goddard SVS. Hindi narration with English captions.
